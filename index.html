Q1 / 5
Question 1 · Alertness

Music wakes you up… for about 20 minutes.

⚡

The boost

Moderate to high volume raises arousal and fights boredom and drowsiness on long, monotonous roads.

Dibben & Williamson; Hargreaves et al.; Ünal et al.
⏳

The fine print

The boost is temporary. Extra vigilance disappears after 15–25 minutes.

Haghani
🔊

Volume

Low volume goes with slower driving. Medium-to-high volume encourages faster driving.

Ghojazadeh et al.; FleetHub

🎷 Genre = brain workload

Complex genres with irregular rhythms (jazz, blues) demand heavy mental processing. Simple structures (instrumental pop) distract far less. Aggressive genres (heavy metal, hard rock) raise heart rate, reduce visual scanning and trigger tailgating and erratic lane changes.

FleetHub; Febriandirza et al.; Youth for Road Safety; Haghani

Question 3 · Confidence

Feels great ≠ drives great.

Mood & enjoyment
Felt in control
Actual safety
Young drivers + their preferred music (illustration of the findings)
  • Brodsky & Slor (BGU): young drivers rate mood and enjoyment much higher with their own music than with calm tracks or silence.
  • But they badly underestimate how distracting it is.
  • The result: a false sense of comfort and an illusion of control. Fast beats push drivers to match their speed to the rhythm and mistake arousal for skill.
Brodsky & Slor, Ben-Gurion University / APS / Accident Analysis & Prevention; Haghani, Univ. of Melbourne; Youth for Road Safety
Q3 / 5
Question 3 · Who stays in control?

Experience is the buffer.

🧑‍✈️

Experienced drivers (25–35)

Have established routines that protect against distraction. They filter out music or switch the radio off in demanding traffic, with no drop in performance.

Dr. Ayça Berfu Ünal, University of Groningen; Haghani
🔰

Novice drivers

Lack this internal regulation. With upbeat, loud or fast music they drift into speeding and miss visual information and road hazards.

Haghani; Brodsky & Slor / APS

🧠 Safe driving = knowing your own cognitive limits and reading the traffic conditions. (Dr. Ünal)

Question 5 · Teenagers

Why teen drivers are the most exposed.

Less-developed processing

Summary & conclusions

It's not if you listen. It's how.

🔊

Volume

Low-to-moderate keeps you alert. Over 80 dB masks sirens and tyres.

🥁

Tempo

60–80 BPM = steady. 120+ BPM = +5–10 km/h and more mistakes.

🎸

Genre & choice

Complex or aggressive genres overload you. Favourite songs pull your attention inward.

🔰

Experience

Seasoned drivers filter it out. Novices and teens can't yet.

Car crashes remain a leading cause of death among young adults. In a world of infotainment screens and endless streaming, many drivers still underestimate the mental load of “just music”.

So what do we do?

Our 5 rules for the road 🛣️

  • Turn it down: stay well below 80 dB so you can hear sirens and horns.
  • Pick calm: songs around 60–80 BPM, like your resting heart rate.
  • Beware the hype songs: your favourites feel great and distract the most.
  • Traffic gets heavy? Music goes off. That's what experienced drivers do.
  • Don't trust the buzz: music's alertness boost fades after 15–25 minutes.
